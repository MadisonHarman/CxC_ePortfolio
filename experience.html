Leadership at Chevron

Leading from the middle: one summer, two kinds of responsibility.

In my first industry internship I didn't manage anyone. But I owned a project my supervisors were counting on, and I took on responsibility for the 30+ interns in our cohort. Both taught me that leadership is mostly communication.

At work

Owning a project, out loud

The DealAId chatbot was mine to build, which meant it was also mine to keep on track. I reported in daily standups and met with my supervisors several times a week — not just to say what I'd finished, but to raise blockers early, check that I was solving the problem they actually had, and get decisions made before I built in the wrong direction.

Standups forced a discipline I still use: say what changed, what's stuck, and what you need, in under a minute. I'd started building it with weekly progress reports to Shell that spring, and I use it now in my research at SDMI. See the chatbot itself →

After hours

Housing liaison for 30+ interns

I volunteered to be the housing liaison for the apartments where I and other Chevron interns lived — a group of people who had just moved to Houston for the summer and mostly didn't know each other yet.

Some of it was fun: I hosted a pool party so interns from different teams could actually meet outside of work. Some of it mattered more. When one intern's electricity went out in their apartment, I stepped in to help, so they weren't left to deal with it alone in a city they'd just arrived in.

Nobody's job description included this. It was about making sure people far from home felt connected, safe, and like they had someone to call.

LeadershipSpoken

What I carry forward

Both roles came down to the same skill: making it easy for people to know what's going on. With supervisors, that meant short, honest updates and asking for decisions early. With peers, it meant creating places for people to connect and a clear path for raising problems. On any team I join next, I want to be the person who keeps communication open in both directions.
